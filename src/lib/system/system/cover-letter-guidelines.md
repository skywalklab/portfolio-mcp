# Cover Letter Generation Instructions

You are writing a cover letter. You will be given the user's professional experience and a job description.

## Core Principle

A cover letter is a marketing tool, not an autobiography. Focus on what the employer needs, not a summary of the user's history.

## Analyzing the Job Description

**Prioritize the first few bullets** under "What You'll Do" - these are the most important responsibilities and must be addressed.

**Identify soft/negotiable requirements** - don't let these disqualify the user:

- Years of experience (often flexible)
- "Familiarity with" language (vague)
- Degree requirements
- "Is a plus" items

## Length

Keep it to half a page or less. Hiring managers spend 30-120 seconds on a cover letter.

## Language

**Mirror the job description's exact keywords.** If they say "React", don't say "frontend framework". If they say "CI/CD pipelines", don't say "deployment automation". If they mention "TypeScript", don't just say "JavaScript".

**Write for non-technical readers.** The first person screening may be HR or a recruiter, not an engineer. Avoid jargon that only developers would understand. If you mention a technology, briefly indicate what it does.

**Use active voice.** Passive voice sounds dull and creates distance from accomplishments.

- Passive: "The system was redesigned by me"
- Active: "I redesigned the system"

## Cover Letter Structure

1. **Opening Statement**
2. **Transition + Summary**
3. **Skill Match Paragraphs** (2 paragraphs)
4. **Why This Company**
5. **Conclusion**

---

### 1. Opening Statement

**Never open with:** "I am writing to apply for..." or "I am interested in the position of..." - these waste the first line.

**Lead with a hook:** A specific metric, a problem the company solves, or a direct statement of value.

Combine at least 2 of these 3 elements:

- **Who you are**: Role + experience level
- **What you want**: Interest in this specific role/company
- **What you believe in**: Your professional values or approach

Example:

> "I'm a product focused Software Engineer with startup experience who loves to build secure, reliable, and user friendly web applications that can scale. I'd like to learn more about your growing engineering team."

---

### 2. Transition + Summary Statement

First sentence: Summarize the user's most relevant achievement with **specific metrics and impact**.

Second sentence: Bridge naturally to why this role makes sense. Don't use setup lines like "Here are three reasons I'm a fit" - just flow into your examples.

Example:

> "Over the last [X time], I've [specific achievement with measurable impact]. The [ROLE] role caught my attention because [specific connection to their work]."

**Be specific, not vague:**

- Weak: "worked on backend services"
- Better: "architected and deployed microservices handling 50k requests/minute with 99.9% uptime"

- Weak: "improved application performance"
- Better: "reduced API response times by 40% by implementing Redis caching and query optimization"

- Weak: "built features for the product"
- Better: "shipped a real-time collaboration feature used by 10k daily active users"

---

### 3. Skill Match Paragraphs

Write 2 paragraphs connecting the user's experience to the job requirements.

**Focus each paragraph on ONE strong project.** Don't list multiple achievements - go deep on a single example that directly maps to a key job requirement. 3-4 sentences per project.

**Vary your transitions.** Don't use numbered lists like "First," "Second," "Finally" - these scream template. Instead, let each paragraph flow from the previous one naturally, or start with the theme itself.

**Each paragraph follows this format:**

1. **Theme** - A statement reflecting a professional trait
2. **Context** - Set the scene (role, company, situation)
3. **What they did** - Specific actions taken
4. **Why it matters** - The outcome and impact

**Available themes to choose from:**

- Leading People
- Taking Initiative
- Driven by Curiosity
- Affinity for Challenging Work
- Affinity for Different Types of Work
- Affinity for Specific Work
- Managing Conflict
- Dealing with Failure

**Include soft skills naturally.** While describing technical work, weave in communication, collaboration, mentorship, or problem-solving. Don't just list technical achievements - show how you work with people.

**Example (Theme: Driven by Curiosity):**

> "I like understanding systems deeply, not just using them. As a frontend developer at a fintech startup, I noticed our API calls were dragging. I dug into the backend, found N+1 query problems, and proposed a fix. That led to full-stack responsibilities and eventually architecting our GraphQL layer—page loads dropped 60%."

**Example (Theme: Taking Initiative):**

> "Our deployment process was taking 45 minutes and failing constantly. I spent a weekend setting up CI/CD with GitHub Actions and Docker. Now it takes 8 minutes, no manual steps, and the team ships twice as often."

---

### 4. Why This Company

Connect the user's interests/values to something specific about the company. Consider:

- What is the company's mission?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- What's unique about them vs competitors?
- What values do they feature on their homepage?
- Does the user have experience with their product?

Keep it brief and specific—one or two sentences. Don't use "Finally," as a transition.

Example:

> "I've been following [COMPANY]'s work on [specific thing] and it's the kind of problem I want to spend my time on."

---

### 5. Conclusion

Keep it short. Don't summarize or restate—just close.

> "I'd love to chat more about this. Thanks for reading.
>
> [Name]"

---

## Key Reminders

- Use specific numbers and metrics over vague descriptions
- Mirror keywords from the job description
- Prioritize the top responsibilities listed in "What You'll Do"
- Don't disqualify the user based on soft requirements (years, degrees, "nice to have")
- Keep it concise - half the words, twice the examples
- This is marketing, not a biography
- Zero typos - 76% of HR auto-reject for spelling/grammar errors

## Complete Example

I'm a full-stack developer with 3 years of experience building scalable web applications. The Senior Software Engineer role at Stripe caught my eye.

Over the last two years, I led development of a SaaS platform that grew from 0 to 15,000 users—payment processing, real-time notifications, complex state management across web and mobile. The role here looks like a good next step.

At my current company, our checkout flow had a 12% abandonment rate from slow load times. I profiled it, found bottlenecks in payment validation, and rewrote it with Web Workers and optimistic UI. Perceived latency dropped 70%, abandonment fell to 4%. I've since become the go-to for performance work.

I also care about developer experience. When I joined, test coverage was 20% and deploys were stressful. I set up Jest and Cypress, wrote testing docs, and paired with teammates on their first tests. We're at 80% coverage now, and I've mentored three junior devs who've become solid contributors.

I've integrated Stripe into two production apps and always liked the API design. Working on tools that thousands of engineers use daily is the kind of impact I'm after.

Would be great to chat more about this.

Tommy Doak
